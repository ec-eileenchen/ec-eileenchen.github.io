Key Insight: Precision-Recall Tradeoff

The model was optimized for higher recall (62.9%) to catch more fraudulent transactions, accepting lower precision (33.1%) to minimize financial losses. This reflects real-world banking priorities where missing fraud is more costly than occasional false positives.

Stakeholder Feedback & Survey Results

Collaboration with the Financial Empowerment Network (FEN) provided crucial insights that shaped the project's direction and implementation.

Survey Methodology

Conducted structured interviews and surveys with FEN staff and community members to understand their needs and evaluate the tool's effectiveness.

Survey Question 1: "How understandable are the fraud detection explanations?"

Results: 85% of respondents found the SHAP-based explanations "clear" or "very clear"

Impact: Confirmed that technical concepts can be effectively translated for non-expert audiences

Survey Question 2: "How important is mobile accessibility for financial education tools?"

Results: 92% rated mobile access as "critical" or "very important"

Impact: Validated the decision to implement mobile-first responsive design

Survey Question 3: "Which language support is most needed in our community?"

Results: Spanish (65%), Chinese (25%), Other (10%)

Impact: Guided prioritization of multi-language implementation

Business Impact & Practical Applications

Reduced Financial Vulnerability

The educational component empowers users to recognize fraud patterns, potentially reducing successful fraud attempts by helping users identify suspicious activity earlier.

Improved Financial Literacy

By explaining how fraud detection works, the project demystifies banking security and builds trust in financial institutions among vulnerable populations.

Scalable Solution

The static web implementation allows FEN to deploy the educational tool without ongoing technical maintenance costs, ensuring long-term sustainability.
